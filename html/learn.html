Species guide

Marsh tick

Ixodes apronophorus

Linked to wet, boggy ground and small mammals. Look for it on walkers and dogs after time near wetlands.

Southern rodent tick

Ixodes acuminatus

Mostly feeds on small rodents in burrows and scrub. Rarely bites people, but can hitch a ride on pets.

Passerine tick

Dermacentor frontalis

Associated with songbirds and ground-feeding birds. Check pets that roam through hedgerows and nesting areas.

Tree-hole tick

Ixodes arboricola

Lives in tree holes and nest boxes where birds roost. More likely near old woodland.

Fox/badger tick

Ixodes canisuga

Found on foxes, badgers and dogs. Common where dogs share ground with wildlife, such as kennels and rural paths.

Prevention tips

Cover up outdoors

Wear long trousers tucked into socks and light colours so ticks are easier to spot in long grass and woodland.

Use repellent

Apply a repellent suitable for skin or clothing. Ask your vet about tick treatments for dogs and cats.

Check daily

Check yourself, children and pets after time outdoors. Ticks like warm, hidden spots: behind knees, armpits, groin, ears and scalp.

Remove ticks promptly

Grip close to the skin with a tick-removal tool or fine tweezers and pull steadily upward. Clean the bite afterwards.

Keep gardens tidy

Mow lawns, clear leaf litter and keep a gravel or wood-chip strip between lawns and woodland.

Know when to get help

See a GP or vet if a rash spreads, or you or your pet feel unwell in the weeks after a bite. Lyme disease is treatable when caught early.
